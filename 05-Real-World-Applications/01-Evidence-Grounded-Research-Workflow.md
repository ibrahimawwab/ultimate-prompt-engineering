# Evidence-Grounded Research Workflow

Prompting can help organize research, but it cannot turn incomplete sources into reliable evidence. A defensible process preserves provenance and separates extraction, interpretation, and conclusions.

## Recommended workflow

1. **Define scope:** write the research question, inclusion criteria, date range, and exclusions.
2. **Collect sources:** use authoritative and relevant sources; record where and when each was obtained.
3. **Extract observations:** capture source statements with exact locators before synthesizing.
4. **Assess source limitations:** consider method, population, recency, conflicts of interest, and missing information.
5. **Synthesize:** group supported findings and explicitly describe disagreement.
6. **Verify:** check each material claim against its source.
7. **Review:** obtain domain-qualified review when the subject or decision warrants it.

## Prompt pattern

> Use only the source excerpts supplied below. For every material factual claim, include the source ID and passage locator. Separate direct source statements from your own synthesis. Do not invent citations, quotations, dates, methods, or missing details. Identify contradictions and gaps. If the supplied evidence does not answer the question, say so. Return: (1) supported findings, (2) conflicting evidence, (3) limitations, and (4) questions requiring human review.

## Verification checklist

- Does every material claim have a traceable source?
- Does the paraphrase preserve scope and qualifications?
- Are publication dates and source metadata accurate?
- Are conflicting findings visible?
- Are unknowns explicitly marked?
- Are recommendations distinguished from evidence?
- Has a human checked the source text?

## Appropriate use

This workflow is suitable for learning, literature triage, and drafting reviewable summaries. It does not replace systematic review methods, expert appraisal, or professional judgment. For medical and scientific work, do not treat generated synthesis as clinical advice or as a substitute for evaluating primary evidence.
